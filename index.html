Antibiotic Resistance

Understanding the Growing Concern

With due respect to:

What is Antibiotic Resistance?

Antibiotic resistance occurs when bacteria develop the ability to survive antibiotics that would normally kill them or stop their growth. This makes certain bacterial infections harder to treat and may require alternative medicines or longer treatment. The unnecessary or inappropriate use of antibiotics contributes to this growing problem. Remember, antibiotics do not work against viruses such as those causing colds and flu. By using antibiotics responsibly, we can help protect their effectiveness for future generations.

8 Important Facts

Small Actions. Big Impact.

Seek Professional Advice

Antibiotics should only be taken when prescribed by a qualified healthcare professional. Not all infections require antibiotics, especially those caused by viruses, such as colds and flu. Taking antibiotics unnecessarily will not help you recover faster and can contribute to antibiotic resistance. Always speak to your doctor or pharmacist if you are unsure.

Follow Directions

Always take antibiotics exactly as prescribed by your doctor or pharmacist. This includes taking the correct dose, at the right time, and for the prescribed duration. Even if you start feeling better, do not stop taking your antibiotics early or continue them longer than directed without professional advice. Following instructions helps ensure your infection is treated effectively.

Never Share Antibiotics

Never share your antibiotics with family members or friends, even if they have similar symptoms. Different infections may require different treatments. Avoid keeping leftover antibiotics for future illnesses or using medicines prescribed previously without medical advice. Using the wrong antibiotic can delay proper treatment, cause unwanted side effects and contribute to antibiotic resistance.

Wash Your Hands

Practising good hand hygiene is one of the simplest ways to prevent infections. Wash your hands regularly with soap and water, especially before eating, after using the toilet and after coughing or sneezing. If soap and water are unavailable, use an appropriate alcohol-based hand sanitiser. Preventing infections reduces the spread of harmful bacteria.

Stay Vaccinated

Keeping your recommended vaccinations up to date helps protect you and your community against certain infections. Vaccines can prevent some bacterial infections directly and reduce viral illnesses that may lead to complications requiring antibiotics. By preventing infections, vaccination can help reduce antibiotic use and limit the development and spread of antibiotic-resistant bacteria.

Dispose Safely

Always return unused, unwanted or expired antibiotics to your local pharmacy for safe disposal. Never throw antibiotics into household rubbish or flush them down the toilet or sink, as medicines can enter the environment and potentially contribute to antimicrobial resistance. Avoid storing leftover antibiotics for future use, as they may not be suitable for your next illness.

Clinical Consequences

Antibiotic resistance can turn once-treatable infections into serious health challenges.
